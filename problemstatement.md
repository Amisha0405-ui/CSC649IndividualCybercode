**1.1 Problem Statement**

Code, debug and cyber threats spotting and learning can be an incredibly frustrating experience for computer science students and self-learners (Cheah, 2020; Dincelli & Femi-Kayode, 2024). The root of the of the problem is that traditional computing classes still depend on static slide and passive lectures, which basically fail to provide students the fast, hands-on practice and real-time error validation which they actually need to learn (Dincelli & Femi-Kayode, 2024; Gwenhure & Nam, 2025). Without active feedback loops or step-by-step guidance to help them learn, beginners often experience mental fatigue and frustration which lead students heavily depend on their lecturers just to get through a lesson (Frank, 2025; Gale & Sentence, 2026). In conclusion, this passive way of teaching fails to build real-world, operational skills that leaves student entirely unprepared to identify live malicious code, phishing scripts or social engineering tactics when they encounter them in real life (Gwenhure & Nam, 2025; EdTech, 2024).

**1.2 Users and Scope** 
-	Primary Users (Computer Science Students & Early Programmers): Computer science undergraduates and beginner programmers who require step-by-step interactive code practice to master syntax, logic and practical debugging without experiencing mental fatigue (Cheah, 2020; Dincelli & Femi-Kayode, 2024).

-	Secondary Users (Self Learners & Computing Enthusiasts): Independent learners who needs step-by-step ways to practice hands-on debugging and real-world threat pattern recognition (Frank, 2025).

-	Secondary Stakeholders (Educators & Instructors): Academic staff who can utilize web-based gamified tools to substitute passive lectures during debugging (Gale & Sentence, 2026).

**1.2.1 Project Scope:**

i)	Interactive Debugging and Fill-in-the-Blank Code Engine (Web IDE): A browser-embedded coding workspace where users detect, reconstruct and complete missing and wrong programming logic, loops, and parameters with immediate syntax feedback.

ii)	Interactive Room-Based Cyber Threat Modules: Scenario-driven virtual rooms focusing on basic cyber threat identification, specifically covering Phishing links analysis, Malware Payload recognition, Online Scams, and Social Engineering tactics.

iii)	Gamified Deterministic Scoring Dashboard: A progress tracking module providing instant code validation, diagnostic hints, performance metrics, and deterministic score evaluation.

**1.2.2 Out-of-Scope:**

i)	Commercial Sandbox Infrastructure: High-level enterprise virtual sandbox environments or full-fledged IDE compilers.

ii)	Automated Vulnerability Scanners: Automated network vulnerability scanning tools, live packet interception, or real-time intrusion detection systems (IDS).

iii)	Advanced Security Frameworks: Penetration testing frameworks, exploit engineering, and advanced cryptographic protocols.

iv)	Targeted System Evaluation: System evaluation is strictly limited to empirical user testing measuring engagement, practical threat pattern recognition, and interface usability.
